Concept of the Day

State over Tokens

Why a model's reasoning trace is computation, not confession

When a reasoning model thinks out loud, the tokens it emits read like an explanation of how it reached an answer. A growing body of evidence shows they are not a faithful record — and a new framework in Nature Machine Intelligence argues we have been reading them the wrong way.

The State over Tokens (SoT) view reframes reasoning tokens not as a linguistic narrative but as an externalized computational state: the scratchpad that carries a model's computation across the separate generation cycles that produce an answer.

Seen this way, the tokens can drive a correct result while still being a poor explanation when read as prose. The text is a carrier of state, not a transcript of intent — which is why a trace that looks plausible can hide reasoning the model never actually performed.

SoT dispels two persistent misconceptions: that the written trace records the complete process, and that the model uses words with the meanings a human reader ascribes to them. Both assumptions quietly underpin how many people interpret and trust model output.

The practical stakes are high. If chains of thought are state rather than confession, then monitoring them as if they were honest narration is unreliable. The paper argues research should move beyond reading the tokens as text and start decoding them as state.
