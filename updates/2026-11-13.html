Concept of the Day

The decision layer: when a model stops writing and starts scoring

Three releases in two days point at the same quiet architectural shift — and it is about cost, not capability

For three years the mental model of a language model has been a writer: you give it a prompt, it gives you prose, and you pay by the token it produces. This week Google shipped a model that mostly refuses to write. Gemini Decision 1, behind the new Decisions API, takes one multimodal input and answers up to a thousand yes/no, multiple-choice or rubric questions in a single forward pass, returning probabilities and confidence scores in 150 to 250 milliseconds. You are billed $0.10 per million input tokens and nothing at all for output, because there is no output to bill.

Perplexity arrived at the same place from the other direction. Decider v1.3 returns numbers for classification, routing, moderation and reranking rather than generating text, and this version adds a Locate feature that hands back the exact span of source text with character offsets. The company reports 91.6% mean accuracy across 63 classification tasks, and it published the weights under Apache-2.0. When both a hyperscaler and a search company converge on the same product shape within a week, that is not a feature launch; it is a layer forming.

The reason is arithmetic. In a typical agent stack, most calls are not the interesting one. They are the router deciding which tool to use, the guardrail deciding whether a request is allowed, the grader deciding whether a retrieved chunk is relevant, the filter deciding whether a draft is safe. Those calls were previously made by asking a chat model to produce a word like yes, and paying for a full generation, a thinking budget and a latency penalty to obtain one bit of information. A model that answers in one pass and charges only for input collapses that cost by one or two orders of magnitude — and, more importantly, collapses the latency, which is what determines whether an agent loop feels instant or broken.

There is a research programme forming around this too. A benchmark called MMDM, released this week, measures what its authors call System-1 decision models: systems that pick among query-specified options in a single pass, with option spaces from 2 to 1,024 and inputs up to roughly 65,000 tokens. It is a test suite for a category that barely had a name six months ago. Meanwhile TypeSafe AI's Jev has been selling the same idea at $0.042 per million input tokens with output free, which tells you the price floor is already being competed on.

The trade-off is interpretability, and it is not small. A model that returns a probability without a rationale is harder to audit for bias, harder to appeal and harder to debug than one that writes a sentence you can disagree with. The TrustMod-SM benchmark published this week found that moderation models which detect more also tend to treat demographic groups less evenly, and that they assign high confidence to wrong predictions and rarely escalate ambiguous cases. If those models become cheap enough to sit inside every request, the disparity scales with them.

So the practical advice for teams building agents this quarter is unglamorous: inventory your calls. Anything that ends in a yes or no, a label, a rank or a route is a candidate for a decision model, and moving it there will cut both cost and latency. Anything where a human will later need to understand why should keep generating text, or keep a decision model and store the span it pointed at. The architecture question of 2026 is not which frontier model you use. It is how much of your traffic you are still paying to have written out in prose.

The State of AI 2026-11-13

Google had the loudest day, releasing Gemini 4 Hydrogen as its new everyday model across the Gemini API, AI Studio, the Gemini app, Search and Gemini for business. It replaces 3.8 Flash, prices flat at $1.50 and $7.50 per million input and output tokens across a million-token context, and Google reports it edging Gemini 4 Argon on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index while more than tripling Terminal-Bench 4.1 scores over the model it replaces. The more consequential detail is Orchestrator in Antigravity, which switches between Carbon and Hydrogen inside one session and reportedly uses 24% less quota. Alongside it, Google put a price on decisions: the Gemini Decisions API charges $0.10 per million input tokens with no output charge for scoring up to a thousand questions in one pass.

The industry story of the day is leadership and money. Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive, with CFO Colette Kress named interim, a departure that landed alongside an antitrust probe and export-control legislation. Nebius guided to about $40 billion of 2027 spending, funded by customer prepayments and contract-backed loans, and then cut its 2026 revenue forecast when Microsoft's New Jersey capacity slipped, sending shares down about 17%. OpenAI's Q3 revenue reportedly passed $10 billion with adjusted losses of $10.9 billion, and Anthropic is on the road with 825 million shares guided at $70 to $78 for a Nasdaq listing under ANT.

On safety and law, five labs were sued in San Francisco by nonprofits seeking a court order to halt frontier development, citing documented harms and the companies' own catastrophic-risk warnings. Australia proposed mandatory audits, published safety frameworks and immediate incident reporting for frontier developers. Anthropic's revised usage policy took effect, banning sustained abuse of models and barring use to decide who gets investigated or charged. An audit found 35,578 Grok-built apps were readable without sign-in, including staff material and a hospital roster.

The research haul was unusually practical. A paper on Deletion Tuning reports that zeroing under 0.05% of a model's weights matches full fine-tuning across RLVR, SFT and on-policy distillation while losing far less of the model's prior skills, and Tree-Ising repairs reasoning loops with bit-level edits for about $0.90 of GPU time. Gemini Robotics 2.5 reported 96% success on automotive parts handling at Mercedes-Benz, and a 72-author FrontierPhysics benchmark found the best configuration clearing fewer than half of 168 attempts on physicist-written problems — a useful corrective to contest-maths scores.

On the tools side, the open-source shelf filled up: a project running 16B models on a first-generation iPad Air with under 1GB of usable memory, Huawei's MACaQ quantizer, Perplexity's Apache-2.0 decision weights, Lynx for building local agent interfaces, and Yogurt for on-device meeting notes. Baidu shipped ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview, a 280B-parameter mixture-of-experts with 14B active and a million-token window, and open-sourced the base model. United Airlines, meanwhile, became the first major consumer brand to bar AI agents from completing a booking.

Research

Tree-Ising repairs reasoning loops with bit-level weight edits for about $0.90 of GPU time

Tree-Ising treats repairing a language model's reasoning loops as a sparse discrete optimisation over its own BF16 weight bits, allowing only sign flips and exponent shifts and splitting the problem into independent per-row QUBO problems solved on GPU. On Qwen3.5-0.8B it reaches 54.6% on MATH-500, against 51.7% for an edit written by a frontier LLM agent and 7.9% for the base model. It needs four forward-pass requests, zero LLM tokens and about $0.90 of GPU time, and on Qwen3.5-2B raises greedy GSM8K accuracy from 21.6% to 69.4%.

TrustMod-SM benchmark finds moderation models trade accuracy for demographic disparity

A preprint introduces TrustMod-SM, a benchmark spanning eight datasets and roughly 29,000 instances that scores language models on trustworthiness in social media moderation across five dimensions: trustfulness, fairness, safety, robustness and context integrity. The authors evaluated 16 open-weight models from 0.5B to 284B parameters plus one closed frontier model, and found persistent gaps: higher detection accuracy often coincides with greater demographic disparity, models frequently comply with concealment or exaggeration prompts, they assign high confidence to wrong predictions, rarely escalate ambiguous cases, and struggle to separate hate speech from counter-speech, especially below 8B parameters. No single model led on all five dimensions.
