Concept of the Day

The cheap layer is where the stack is being decided

Why fast models, routers and stopping rules now matter more than another point on a benchmark

For two years the question was which model was smartest. This week the releases answered a different question: which model is cheapest to run at volume, and how does software choose between them mid-task. Gemini 4 Hydrogen lands near its larger sibling on an intelligence index, runs roughly 2.5 times faster than the model it replaces, and costs a fraction of a flagship. Kimi K3.1 Pro Highspeed makes the same trade explicit by shipping a fast twin of the same model. Anthropic's Fable 5.2 lowers its per-token price while adding an optional fast mode. Capability stopped being the scarce input; tokens per second per rupee became the thing to optimise.

Once several models are good enough, the interesting engineering moves up a level. Google's Antigravity Orchestrator switches between Hydrogen and Carbon inside one session and claims 24% less quota for near-Carbon results. Google's new Decisions API skips text generation entirely and returns up to a thousand scored yes/no answers per request for a tenth of a dollar per million input tokens. Those two products are the same idea from different angles: a cheap, boring call that decides what the expensive call should do.

Research is converging on the same conclusion from the measurement side. SO-Arena, a new benchmark for models that answer in a single pass by choosing among options, found that the most accurate system ranked only seventh on reliability, meaning it changed its answer when the option set shifted. A separate study of long-horizon agents found that decision models used as judges with hindsight credited useful steps far better than the same models used as actors, and that using them as step-level teachers lifted a small policy from 72.8% to 82.9% on ALFWorld. The cheap layer is not just a cost saving; it is a better place to put the judgement.

The most expensive finding of the week is about stopping. Termination Lag measured coding agents across five models and three benchmarks and found that 38 to 82% of successful runs reach a correct patch on their first edit, after which the remaining steps take a median 35 to 60% of the run, mostly spent re-verifying and exploring. In 1 to 8% of cases the agent then undoes its own correct fix. You are paying a third to over half of your agent budget to watch it talk itself out of the right answer.

Put together, the practical advice for anyone shipping agents this month is unglamorous. Benchmark the fast tier on your own tasks before believing a vendor chart, put a cheap decision model in front of the expensive one, and spend real effort on the stop condition. Edge0's laptop-scale 145B model and Hunyuan's 770B open release widen the options further, though a missing licence and unverified benchmarks are exactly the details that decide whether you can use them in production.

None of this makes safety easier. The same week that agents got a payment token and enterprises got a self-hosted control plane, a leaked vendor key put a poisoned coding extension in front of about 5,000 developers, and four nonprofits asked a California court to freeze frontier development altogether. The layer that decides what an agent does next is also the layer where you can enforce what it may never do, which is why routing and guardrails are turning out to be the same project.

The State of AI 2026-11-15

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 on one underlying model, cutting Fable's API price to $8 and $40 per million tokens and reserving Mythos for vetted cyber-defence users. Google answered with Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a fast daily-driver model at $1.50 and $7.50 per million tokens with a million-token window, rolling out across the API, AI Studio, Search and Antigravity with an Orchestrator that swaps it for the larger Carbon mid-session.

The open-weight side was just as busy: Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro with a million-token context, Meituan's 1.6-trillion-parameter LongCat-2.5 with 48 billion active, Tencent's 770-billion-parameter Hunyuan Hy4 with computer operation, Alibaba's quiet Qwen3.5 launch, and Edge0's 145-billion-parameter model that runs on a laptop with no licence named yet.

Money and policy moved faster than the models. Anthropic's IPO roadshow is winding up with compute commitments at the centre of the story and Dario Amodei calling pre-buying capacity his deepest business dilemma, while AirTrunk signed an MoU for 1.8GW of data centre capacity in Andhra Pradesh. Nvidia is being run by an interim chief executive after Jensen Huang's resignation, with a petition from tech executives demanding reinstatement and chip export scrutiny heading to Congress in December.

The week's sharpest safety items were concrete rather than theoretical. Cursor's post-mortem showed a leaked vendor key was enough to place a poisoned Claude Code extension in front of about 5,000 developers, and four nonprofits sued OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and SpaceX in San Francisco to bar any model more capable than their current ones as of 12 November. The FAA's first nationwide ground stop since 2023 also came days after its AI-assisted routing system went live nationwide, with no public log to confirm or rule out a link.

On the research side, a benchmark of System One decision models found the most accurate one ranked seventh on reliability, a study of coding agents found a median 35 to 60% of successful runs is spent after the correct patch is already in place, and Together AI described a compiler that lets an agent loop keep re-optimising inference as models and traffic shift. Read today's concept for why the cheap layer between you and the model is where the stack is actually being decided.

The FAA halted departures nationwide on 13 November after what the Transportation Secretary called a communication issue affecting all air route traffic control centres, with roughly 7,000 flights delayed or cancelled. Days earlier the agency had finished expanding SMART, an AI-assisted airspace routing system built by contractor Air Space Intelligence under a deal worth just under $1 billion, from a Washington pilot to every en route centre. The company says its products did not cause the ground stop, and a subsequent account attributes the outage to a router overload during a routine fibre upgrade at a remote site.

Benchmark with injected errors finds AI reviewers uneven and prone to missing mistakes

Researchers built a benchmark that inserts four kinds of clear mistakes into real papers at three severity levels, then measured how well reviewer systems catch them and how often they flag clean papers. On an astrophysics set a multi-agent system called Skepthical found about 70% of injected errors, the best result, while on a machine-learning set it tied the strongest single model at roughly 81%. Structured prompts improved detection by up to 48 percentage points for top models, and false-positive rates stayed under 10% for most systems, mostly from wrongly flagging maths or numeric claims.

Together Compiler: an agent loop that continuously compiles inference workloads

Together AI published Together Compiler (TKCC), a continual agent loop for inference optimisation that separates engine launch overhead from forward-pass computation with a tiered persistent engine, and uses a cost model to pick parallelism configurations. The company reports up to 1.7x over hand-written megakernels on a Llama 1B workload and up to 2.6x higher throughput on frontier models including Kimi K3, GLM 5.3, MiniMax M3.1 Flash and DeepSeek v4.1 Flash, with 1,111 tokens per second at batch size one on M3.1 Flash.
