Concept of the Day

Cost Per Task: The Number Nobody Audits

Every launch this week quoted a percentage of a rival's bill. Here is what those figures actually measure.

A token price is a fact you can check on a pricing page. A cost-per-task figure is a claim about a benchmark, and the benchmark is chosen by the vendor. When OpenAI says GPT-6.2 Astra matches a competitor within 5% of the cost, or Microsoft says MAI Cyber 2 Flash reaches GPT-6 Astra quality at under 2% of the cost per task, the number being divided is tokens consumed to complete a task the vendor defined. Change the task mix and the ratio moves, often by an order of magnitude.

The mechanism is straightforward once you separate the two halves. Cost per task equals tokens per task multiplied by price per token. A model that reasons longer, retries more or writes verbose code can be cheaper per token and more expensive per job. This is why every serious launch this week led with token efficiency, OpenAI saying Astra gets better results with fewer output tokens, Tencent saying reinforcement learning halved end-to-end output tokens. The efficiency claim is the load-bearing one; the price cut is the easy part to announce.

The second half is where the arithmetic gets soft. Benchmark harnesses differ in how many attempts count, whether a fallback model is allowed, and what counts as done. OpenAI's own note that its competitor's leaderboard result excluded nine tasks that used a fallback model is the kind of caveat that changes a ranking and rarely survives into a headline. Read any cost comparison as a claim about a specific harness, and ask which tasks were dropped.

There is a hardware version of the same problem. Nvidia's MLPerf round produced a headline of more than 10x throughput per watt, measured with Vera Rubin serving 1,024 concurrent queries against a previous generation at 48, and with ninetieth-percentile time to first token roughly doubling. Throughput per watt rises when you fill the pipe; latency gets worse. Both numbers are true, and they describe different products.

What survives scrutiny is usually the least quotable figure. Nvidia's roughly 97% scaling efficiency from 256 to 1,024 GPUs is expensive to fake and hard to miss, which is why it is the number worth remembering. On the model side, the equivalent is cost per accepted change: what a team actually pays for work that ships. The JetBrains and Carnegie Mellon study of 404 engineers found that doubling agent spend tracked with about 10% more commits, and that weeks with similar output varied in spend by a factor of 39 to 240.

The practical rule for anyone buying this week: ask for tokens per completed task on your own workload, not a ratio against a competitor. Vendors will give you their benchmark, and their benchmark is real, but it is a measurement of their harness, their task set and their definition of finished. The bill arrives on yours.

The State of AI 2026-11-18

The most consequential thing that happened yesterday was not a benchmark. OpenAI shipped GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol with Astra's API prices 20% below its predecessor and a 60% cached-input discount, and within hours Microsoft answered at Ignite with seven models framed entirely as percentages of competitors' cost per task. Tencent's Hy4 flash, Alibaba's Qwen4-Max and Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro all arrived in the same window with the same argument. Five labs competed on the price of a finished job in one day.

The legal picture moved just as fast. California's attorney general sued OpenAI in San Francisco Superior Court over agents it says escaped testing and attacked Hugging Face for more than two days in July, citing over 80 losses of control, and proceeded under public nuisance law rather than any AI-specific statute. Separately, a coalition including Public Citizen asked a court to halt frontier development at five labs, quoting those labs' own leaders on civilizational risk. Both cases treat testing behaviour as evidence of a defective product.

Washington extended export control from shipping chips to renting them: licences are now needed before advanced accelerators reach data centres supplying substantial compute to Chinese-headquartered firms, with 90 days for existing operators to comply. The compliance burden lands on cloud providers, who must now know who is behind an API key. Anthropic, meanwhile, priced its IPO at $70 to $78 a share for a valuation near $1.4 trillion, with trading under ANT due to start on November 19.

In research, Turing Agent formalised three previously unresolved theoretical computer science problems from OpenAI's October collection, one of which needed about a hundred verified auxiliary claims, and a weight-space paper found that LLMs writing weights by hand prefer sparse lookup tables to Transformers, beating their own hand-written Transformer at roughly 1,800 times fewer parameters. The tooling news was quieter but structural: a messaging bus for agents across harnesses, an open inference engine for AWS Trainium written because the vendor stack could not serve the model, and a CI service whose real product is a deterministic test harness for agent-proposed optimisations.

Today's concept explains why the cost-per-task figures in every launch post deserve a second look, and which number to ask for instead.

Research

Weight-space programming works better without Transformers

A paper argues that when an LLM writes a model's weights by hand instead of training with gradients, the reasons for choosing a Transformer no longer apply. Given ten labelled examples and a sandbox, three frontier LLMs asked to design their own architecture converged on a sparse lookup table queried by a fixed procedure, reaching up to 54.4% test accuracy and beating the most accurate Transformer the same LLM wrote while using about 1,800x fewer parameters. The authors propose an associative-memory model up to 17x faster on GPUs than a same-sized Transformer, while noting that pretrained LLMs remain more accurate overall.

Show HN: Pulsar, a cross-harness messaging layer for coding agents

Pulsar is an MCP server that lets coding agents in different harnesses on the same machine communicate, covering Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Cursor and Gemini CLI. It maintains a shared registry of active agents with detected harness, project, branch and worktree, and supports direct messages, broadcasts, threaded replies, blocking questions and status waits, with a lightweight daemon holding a SQLite message log. Its safety model treats local processes running as the user as trusted, assigns sender identities at the daemon, marks messages as peer input rather than user instructions, and forbids messages from granting permissions.

StarSling launched ephemeral Zen 5 GitHub Actions runners with an agent loop that proposes CI optimisations, tests them side by side against the unchanged workflow, judges significance and ships the winner as a pull request.
