Concept of the Day

The Verification Gap

Why every number in AI news now arrives with an asterisk about who checked it

The most interesting thing about this week's AI news is not any single release. It is that almost every headline came with a note about who verified it, and in several cases the answer was: the company that shipped it.

Start with the cryptanalysis result. Google DeepMind's agents, working with academic cryptographers, found two weaknesses in legacy 1024-bit RSA, and the reported speedups are striking: roughly 1,600 times faster signature forgery where an attacker controls a short fingerprint, and about 30 times cheaper bulk factoring per additional key. Both multipliers are theoretical rather than measured end to end. What actually ran was a sandboxed reproduction against a key an academic team had already broken, where the agents used 3.5 times less CPU time than factoring it outright.

Then take METR's eleven-week assessment of Anthropic. It estimates capability gains running about 1.9 times faster than the prior period, with roughly 80 percent attributed to AI assisting research rather than added compute, a number METR itself calls a subjective judgment. The sharper fact is what is missing: Anthropic held unilateral redaction rights and cut a majority of the evidence METR found most informative, and a planned check on the capability-versus-safety compute split was cancelled partway through.

The pattern repeats in the model releases. Moonshot's 65.7 on Terminal-Bench 4.0 sits within a point of Claude Opus 5.5 on Moonshot's own runs; the independent check, Artificial Analysis at 55 on its Intelligence Index, is three points behind rather than one. Meta's Muse Spark Open table shows strong MCP-Atlas and JobBench numbers and an Intelligence Index of 39, behind GLM 5.3, Kimi K3 and Qwen3.8 in the same table. Ornith's 84.5 on SWE-bench Verified is a claim with released artefacts, not a reproduced result.

None of this means the capability is fake. It means the measurement layer has not kept up with the release layer. Vendors ship faster than evaluators can audit, evaluators depend on vendor access, and the audits that do happen arrive partly redacted. The practical consequence for anyone making a decision is to ask a boring question of every claim: what number did I measure myself, on my own workload, this month?

The one place independent checking looked strongest this week was also the most uncomfortable. Vals AI ran agents against simulated corporate networks and found the open-weight DeepSeek V4.1 Flash reaching 72.8 percent of attack objectives against 80.8 percent for a restricted frontier model, at a tenth of the cost, and noted that open models do most of their work after 100 million tokens so short tests understate them. The gap is narrowing, and the measurement window is short.

The State of AI 2026-11-08

The open-weight side had its strongest week of the year. Moonshot published the full weights of Kimi K3.1 Pro, a 2.8-trillion-parameter model that lands three points behind Claude Opus 5.5 on Artificial Analysis's index, while Meta shipped Muse Spark Open at 816 billion parameters, Baidu released a 280-billion-parameter base model with no instruction tuning at all, and Ornith put out an MIT-licensed 122B coding model claiming 84.5 on SWE-bench Verified.

Anthropic is pressing ahead with a pre-Thanksgiving IPO at a $1.25 to $1.75 trillion valuation, below the $2 trillion some investors wanted, and OpenAI closed $11 billion of a round it had once sized at $30 billion. Google bought Crusoe's unfinished Wyoming data centre site outright and plans $17 billion of investment there, a sign that building at this scale increasingly requires a hyperscaler balance sheet.

On the research side, NVIDIA's VFlash predicts whole robot trajectories with a 537-million-parameter diffusion drafter for roughly 25x faster planning, Recursive Sparse Attention hits 60.5 on RULER at a million tokens after training at 32K, and an agent called KernelSeek claims CUDA kernels faster than hand-tuned FlashAttention, with kernels and traces released for checking.

Safety news ran through the same verification question. OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber ships with a Critical cybersecurity rating behind gated Daybreak Red access, Vals AI found the cyber gap between open-weight and closed agents narrowing fast, and DeepMind's RSA result is real but its headline multipliers are theoretical. The details that decide how much any of it matters are in the caveats, not the numbers.

If you read one thing today, read the METR assessment of Anthropic alongside DeepMind's RSA writeup. Together they show the shape of the current problem: capability is advancing faster than the institutions that check it, and the checks that do happen arrive partly redacted. That is the concept of the day below.

KernelSeek agent claims faster CUDA FlashAttention kernels than published implementations

A preprint by Gabriele Oliaro and colleagues introduces KernelSeek, a question-driven coding agent that asks brief, targeted questions at decision points instead of loading the model with parsed profilers or heavy tool stacks. On the reported tasks it improved a FlashAttention-3 kernel to 1.13 times baseline and produced single-file, dependency-free forward kernels for Hopper and Blackwell that the authors say are correct on all 182 tested scenarios per architecture, yielding 1.16 times FlashAttention-3 and 1.46 times FlashAttention-4 on geometric mean. Kernels, harnesses and traces were released.
