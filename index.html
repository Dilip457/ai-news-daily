Concept of the Day

Diffusion Language Models

Why Inception's Mercury 3 writes a whole answer in parallel instead of one token at a time

Almost every large language model you have used is autoregressive: it predicts the next token, appends it, and repeats. The sequence is generated strictly left to right, and the model cannot revise token three after it has already committed token four. That constraint is why generation feels like typing, and why latency scales roughly with the length of the answer.

A diffusion language model breaks that ordering. It starts from a rough, noisy draft of the entire response and refines every position at once over a handful of passes, the same way image diffusion models turn static into a picture. Inception's Mercury 3 uses this approach to reach around 1,068 tokens per second on Nvidia GPUs, where a conventional model like Claude Haiku 5.5 manages roughly 240 in the same comparison.

The attraction is latency, not raw quality. A voice agent, a live search answer or an autocomplete in an editor is judged in hundreds of milliseconds, and a model that fills in a whole paragraph in parallel can feel instant where a token-by-token model feels sluggish. Inception's early customers put Mercury 3 into smart glasses and tutoring tools, exactly the places where a five-second pause reads as broken.

The trade-off is that diffusion decoders have historically trailed autoregressive models on hard reasoning, because refining all positions together gives less room for the careful, sequential chain that complex problems reward. Inception claims its own benchmarks close much of that gap, leading on eight of twelve tests, but its results are self-reported and no outside group has reproduced them yet.

There is also a practical asymmetry: Mercury 3 is API-only, with no open weights, so the speed advantage is rented rather than owned. That is the opposite strategy to the open inference engines also shipping this week, and it sets up a clear question for buyers: do you want the fastest tokens, or the ones you can run yourself?

Keep the concept, not the brand. Diffusion decoding is now a genuine second lane next to autoregressive generation, and the fact that a startup can match mid-tier frontier models while running several times faster is the strongest argument yet that the next fight in LLMs is over how tokens are produced, not just how good they are.
