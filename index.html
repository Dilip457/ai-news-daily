Concept of the Day

The decision tier

Why the cheapest AI work is no longer text generation

For three years the mental model of an LLM call was simple: you send text, you get text, and you pay for both. That model quietly assumed every call was a small act of writing. But look at what an agent actually does all day and the picture changes. It decides whether a retrieved document is relevant, which tool to call, whether a policy check passes, how to route a request to a cheaper model, and whether a draft is good enough to ship. Almost none of that needs prose. It needs a label and a confidence number.

Google's answer this week was to price that reality directly. Gemini Decision 1 and the Decisions API take a multimodal input and up to a thousand closed-ended questions, and return probabilities with a confidence score for each. You are billed $0.10 per million input tokens and nothing for output tokens, because there is no output to generate. Meta's Muse Ember makes the same argument from the other direction: a model priced at ten cents per million input tokens, explicitly tuned for tool calls, coding subtasks, routing and extraction.

The economics follow from a structural fact about how transformers work. Generating a token requires a forward pass through the whole model for every token produced; classifying does not. A model that emits a single probability vector over a fixed set of options spends its compute once, on the input. This is why a decision endpoint can quote a median latency of 150 to 250 milliseconds while a generative endpoint of comparable quality cannot. The saving is not a discount, it is an architectural consequence.

There is a second consequence that matters more for reliability. A generative model asked to answer yes or no in text can produce an ambiguous answer, a hedge, or a paragraph you then have to parse with a regex. A calibrated probability has a well-defined meaning, and the confidence score lets you set a threshold: route the confident cases automatically and send the rest to a fallback model or a human. Ambiguity becomes a number you can act on rather than a sentence you have to interpret.

The catch is calibration. A confidence score is only useful if 0.9 really means ninety percent of those cases are correct, and calibration drifts when you change the prompt, the input distribution or the model version. Teams adopting a decision endpoint should measure reliability on their own traffic rather than trusting a vendor's headline latency, and should keep a labelled evaluation set for exactly this. The cheaper the call, the easier it is to run that evaluation on every request.

Watch where this leaves the middle of the market. If routing and extraction move to a ten-cent model and hard reasoning moves to a premium model that spends tokens exploring, the mid-priced generalist has the least defensible position. The decision tier is not a feature of one product; it is a reorganisation of what gets priced how.

The State of AI 2026-11-12

The biggest story of the last 24 hours is money, not models. Anthropic launched its IPO roadshow at $70 to $78 a share, 825 million Class A shares on Nasdaq as ANT, implying about $1.4 trillion at the top of the range and up to roughly $64 billion raised. The same filing describes a $26 billion private placement of non-voting stock at the IPO price to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC, with Google and Nvidia reportedly taking $10 billion each. Pricing is expected November 18.

OpenAI's numbers landed alongside. Quarterly revenue crossed $10 billion for the first time, up almost 50 percent from $6.7 billion, with 100 million paying subscribers, against a $10.9 billion adjusted operating loss. Anthropic's revenue over the same period was higher at nearly $17 billion. Both companies are growing fast and losing fast, which is the argument each will have to make to public markets within months of each other.

On the model side, Google made Gemini 4 Carbon generally available at Argon's introductory rate of $2 and $10 per million tokens until November 30, then $4 and $20, and shipped Gemini Decision 1 with an API that bills no output tokens at all. Meituan released LongCat-2.5 at 1.6 trillion parameters under an MIT licence, Moonshot shipped Kimi K3.1 Pro at 2.8 trillion with 104 billion active, and Baidu put out ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview with Apache 2.0 weights promised.

The research that will age best is the verification work. DeepMind's AlphaProof Code agents wrote more than six million lines of Lean proofs checking security properties of the compiled code behind Signal's protocol in about four weeks, catching two deliberately reintroduced bugs, with artefacts to be open-sourced. AlphaGenome 2 resolves DNA variant effects per cell type rather than per tissue, and Gemini Robotics 2.5 posted a 96 percent success rate on live Mercedes-Benz lines in Hungary and Germany.

Finally, the safety ledger got heavier. Fireworks AI widened its October breach disclosure to include billing data and partial model weights, New York opened RAISE Act registration with a January 2027 deadline and a 72-hour incident clock, and Australia said it will consider a criminal offence for AI companies that fail to take reasonable steps against harmful model conduct. Read the concept below for why the cheapest AI work is no longer writing.

Research

Transformers provably learn depth-first search, with depth buying exponential look-ahead

A theory paper analyses path-finding on directed graphs as a symbolic stand-in for multi-step reasoning. It proves that one-layer transformers need quadratic size on dense graphs to implement the key DFS child-selection primitive, while two layers suffice with O(N log N) size. For every k of at least 3, a k-layer transformer implements bounded-reachability DFS with a look-ahead horizon of 1 plus 2 to the power k minus 3, an exponential increase with depth. REAL Loss fixes a gradient-sign bug in speculative decoding training

Speculative decoding speedup is governed by expected acceptance length, but standard block-level training objectives are surrogates that average later acceptance over target-distributed continuations rather than the verification branches actually selected by accepted tokens. The authors show this introduces a covariance gap that can reverse the gradient direction, then derive REAL, an unbiased loss using path weights with Rao-Blackwellisation for variance reduction. With a Qwen3.6-35B-A3B target and a Domino drafter it outperforms WTV, VSD, DBLast and a static baseline across nine task subsets and five seeds, with no change to architecture or inference.
