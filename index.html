Concept of the Day

Hybrid Reasoning Agents

Why the next generation of agents will think in silence and speak only to act.

Every agent you use today narrates its thinking. It writes out each reasoning step as text before it searches, runs code or takes an action, and you pay for all of it — in tokens, in latency and in the context window those steps consume. Hybrid Reasoning Agents, a new paper from Stanford, Together AI and colleagues, asks a simple question: why should deliberation be written down at all, when only the action and the answer need to leave the model?

Their design keeps reasoning inside the model's latent space — the continuous internal representation it already uses — and switches to plain text only when it has to call a tool or deliver a final answer. A lightweight component called HybridLink moves the model between latent and text modes within a single generation process, and a continuous working memory carries both kinds of context across turns. Tool calls stay in ordinary text, so the steps that matter for auditing remain inspectable.

Training happens in two stages. HybridSFT teaches the model to work with HybridLink and to interleave tool use with integrated generation; HybridRL then pushes it to prefer thinking in latent space when it can and switching to text when it must act. Across three model scales and eleven benchmarks spanning maths, science, medicine, search and agentic reasoning, the hybrid agents beat frontier LLM agents and existing agentic frameworks by an average of 10.4%, with a 14B model outperforming the strongest agentic baseline by 17.4% on agentic tasks.

The headline number for anyone running agents in production is not the accuracy gain but the cost: 35.5% to 54.7% fewer tokens. Long chains of visible reasoning are the default cost centre in agent stacks, and simply not writing them out removes a large slice of both the bill and the wait before the first useful action.

There is a real trade-off, and the authors are honest about it. Externalised chain-of-thought is the easiest thing to monitor, and as more systems move reasoning inward, the standard assumption that you can read what a model is thinking breaks down. That tension — cheaper, faster agents versus monitorable ones — is the design question this work puts on the table, and it is the one safety and platform teams will be arguing about next.

Hybrid Reasoning Agents switch between latent-space thinking and text to cut agent token costs by up to 55%

Researchers from Stanford, Together AI and colleagues introduced Hybrid Reasoning Agents, which reason internally in latent space and switch to text only to call a tool or give an answer, using a lightweight HybridLink and a continuous working memory. Trained with HybridSFT and HybridRL, they improved over frontier LLM agents by 10.4% on average across three model scales and 11 benchmarks, with 35.5%-54.7% token savings, and a 14B model beat the strongest agentic baseline by 17.4% on agentic tasks.

NVIDIA publishes an open 'midtraining' recipe that measurably improves coding agents

An NVIDIA team published a detailed recipe for midtraining, a stage between pretraining and SFT/RL that exposes base models to long structured artefacts such as whole repositories, pull requests and build logs, plus a bounded share of agent trajectories. Training at a 256,000-token context with roughly 10-15% agent traces lifted Nemotron 3.5 Lightning's SWE-bench Verified from 60.7% to 64.5% and Nemotron 3.5 Super's DeepSWE from 11.7% to 15.7% after identical SFT. The authors flag single-family, mostly coding-focused limits.

Google opens Deep Review to the public to verify technical documents at scale

After piloting its Paper Assistant Tool across more than 16,000 submissions at STOC, ICML, NeurIPS and ICLR, Google is launching Deep Review publicly. The agentic system segments a document, allocates compute by information density, runs parallel verification agents with full-document context, then synthesises and grounds its findings, and it is expanding from scientific papers to legal, financial and design documents. Every user gets two free reviews a week, with a Gemini API key for higher limits.

Nature 'byteification' retrofit turns OLMo, Qwen3 and Llama into byte-level models on under 1% of a pretraining budget

A group spanning Cambridge, Edinburgh and the Allen Institute for AI published a two-stage retrofit in Nature that converts existing subword language models into byte-level ones for less than 1% of a typical pretraining budget, totalling 49.1B tokens across two stages. Bolmo 7B, built from OLMo 3, posts a +16.5% absolute improvement on STEM tasks over the current byte-level baseline, and the recipe shipped four variants including Bwen 8B from Qwen3 and Blama 8B from Llama 3.
