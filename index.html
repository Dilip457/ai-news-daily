Concept of the Day

Diffusion Language Models

Why generating text by denoising instead of left-to-right changes the economics of inference

Every mainstream large language model you have used is autoregressive: it writes one token at a time, and each token is conditioned on everything that came before it. That left-to-right constraint is what makes chat feel fluent, and it is also the reason generation is hard to parallelise. The model cannot decide token ten until token nine exists, so speed is bounded by how fast a single sequence of dependent steps can run.

A diffusion language model takes a different route. Instead of emitting tokens in order, it starts from noise and iteratively denoises a whole block of tokens at once, refining the entire sequence over a handful of passes. Because the tokens in a block are predicted jointly rather than one after another, the work can be spread across far more of the accelerator in parallel, which is where the dramatic throughput numbers come from.

Inception's Mercury 3, released on October 19, is the clearest test of whether this approach scales. It is more than forty times larger than Mercury 1, yet still generates over 1,000 tokens per second on commodity Nvidia GPUs, and Inception claims quality on par with frontier speed-optimised autoregressive models. That combination, more capable and faster, is exactly what diffusion sceptics said could not happen.

The trade-offs are real, though. Diffusion models need a schedule of denoising steps, and the quality-speed curve depends on how that schedule is tuned and how much context is held in the denoised block. Early diffusion models lagged autoregressive ones on reasoning-heavy tasks, and independent verification of vendor benchmarks is still thin. The scaling laws that carry autoregressive models may hold here too, as Inception argues, but they are being established in public in real time.

The practical upshot is that inference economics is becoming a first-class design axis. When a model can answer at a thousand tokens a second for a tenth of a cent, latency-sensitive products like browser-use agents, real-time search and live tutoring become viable in ways they were not before. Diffusion is one of several routes to that future, alongside aggressive sparsity, distillation and small specialised models, and it is the one that most directly challenges the assumption that intelligence must come at the cost of speed.

Xiaomi opens Lens-0, a 4B multimodal LLM that predicts region masks

Xiaomi's technical report introduces Xiaomi-Lens-0, a 4B multimodal LLM built on Qwen3.5 that decodes region masks directly from the shared vision encoder's patch-level features, removing the need for a separate segmentation head. It averages +5.6 points over the Qwen3.5 baseline on REC splits and tops cIoU on all eight RefCOCO/+/g RES splits, alongside a new human-reviewed LensBench grounding benchmark.

IBM integrates the Spyre accelerator into PyTorch

IBM detailed Torch-Spyre, an open-source project that provides a PyTorch PrivateUse1 device with an Inductor backend for the IBM Spyre accelerator, a 32-core energy-efficient AI chip with compiler-managed scratchpad memory. IBM says Spyre can now run thousands of models from Hugging Face and vLLM through the PyTorch integration.
