Concept of the Day

Decision Models

The cheap, fast plumbing that decides whether an AI acts

Most AI products you use are built from models that generate text. A newer and quieter category does the opposite: you hand it a situation and a fixed set of options, and it returns a calibrated probability for each one. It never writes a sentence, never samples a token, and costs a fraction of a chat model to run. These are decision models, and this week they went from curiosity to a crowded field.

The job they do is routing, classification, verification and control — the small, high-volume judgements an agent makes constantly. Should this request go to a big model or a small one? Is this output safe to ship? Does this tool call need approval? Today those calls are often made by an expensive LLM or a brittle rule. A decision model makes them in a single forward pass, deterministically, at a price measured in hundredths of a cent.

The week's clearest signal was Microsoft's Decision-1, a small model that reads a state and options and returns probabilities, built not on OpenAI's stack but on Alibaba's open-weights Qwen3.5-9B. Microsoft matched the category leader Jev's price exactly and says it is already routing work across Copilot and Xbox. Nace AI open-sourced Drex 1.5, a 9B model that tops the public Decision Index under 10B parameters, and Cloudflare added Clef-omni, which extends the same idea to audio, images and video.

Why now? Because the agent economy is bottlenecked on exactly these micro-decisions, and because open weights have made the base models cheap enough that a competent lab can post-train a competitive decision model in weeks. When a 9B model can be fine-tuned for the price of a few days of rented GPU time, the moat is not the model — it is distribution and price.

The strategic undertow matters as much as the technology. Microsoft choosing Qwen over its own partner, days after OpenAI opened a rival Decisions API, tells you that the layer deciding whether an agent acts is becoming too important to outsource. If decision models are the plumbing of agentic software, whoever owns the plumbing owns the flow — and that is a quieter contest than the race for the biggest frontier model.

TU Darmstadt's U-Space tracks token-level model uncertainty with zero extra sampling

Researchers at TU Darmstadt introduced U-Space, a mechanistic-interpretability framework that identifies semantic anchors of doubt and certainty in a model's unembedding matrix and builds an orthogonal subspace in the residual stream. The accompanying U-Lens projects token activations onto that basis to give a real-time, token-level uncertainty trajectory with no training, no labels and no repeated generations. It outperforms sampling and probe baselines under length-controlled settings and cuts uncertainty-estimation compute by over 90%.

Research

PRICE: adaptive rollouts and voting to hit LLM accuracy under a token budget

A new paper shows that test-time accuracy under a fixed token budget is best served by adapting both the number of rollouts and the voting rule per query, rather than keeping the voting rule fixed. It unifies self-consistency and best-of-n as special cases of a Boltzmann-weighted vote family and proposes PRICE, an algorithm that makes the joint decision at query level.
