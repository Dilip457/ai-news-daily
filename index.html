Concept of the Day

Mixture-of-Experts: the architecture behind today's cheap frontier models

Why Kimi K3, Ling 3.1 Flash, Solar Mini 4 and DeepSeek's new flash all activate only a slice of their parameters per token.

Every model in today's briefing that advertises frontier performance at a non-frontier price has the same structural trick underneath it. Moonshot's Kimi K3 carries roughly 2.8 trillion parameters. inclusionAI's Ling 3.1 Flash has 560 billion. Upstage's Solar Mini 4 has 35 billion. None of them runs all of those weights for a given token.

A mixture-of-experts model splits its network into many specialised sub-networks, called experts, and adds a small routing network that decides, for each token, which handful of experts should handle it. Solar Mini 4 activates about 3 billion of its 35 billion parameters per token; Ling 3.1 Flash activates about 25 billion of 560 billion. The full parameter count is the model's capacity, while the active count is what it actually costs to run each step.

That split is the whole point. Training and inference cost scale mainly with active parameters, so a sparse model can carry a vast amount of stored knowledge without paying to compute over all of it at once. The result is a model that behaves like something much larger while being served like something much smaller, which is exactly how a 560B open-weight model can be offered free on a trial endpoint.

The trade-off is that sparse routing is harder to train and harder to serve reliably. Expert capacity is finite, so if traffic concentrates on a few experts the model can drop tokens or slow down, and the router itself must learn to assign work sensibly without any supervision telling it which expert is right. That is why the gains are real but uneven, and why benchmark cards from these labs tend to be published while the weights and pricing are not.

Watch the active-parameter number, not the headline total, when you compare these models. A 35B model activating 3B and a 560B model activating 25B are closer in running cost than their total sizes suggest, and both can outperform a dense model with a far larger active footprint on the tasks their training targeted. The parameter total is marketing; the active count is the invoice.

Creatify Labs launches Boreal-H3, a video model post-trained for advertising

Creatify Labs launched Boreal-H3, an AI video model for advertising built by post-training MiniMax H3 in partnership with MiniMax. It scores 133.3 on Creatify's Ads Quality Index against the base model's 100, and costs $0.04 per second at 768p, about one-twelfth the price of Seedance 2.5. It is available in Creatify's playground and API with text-to-video, image-to-video and reference-to-video modes.
