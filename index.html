Concept of the Day

The Task, Not the Token

Why cost per completed job just became the only AI benchmark that matters

For three years the industry measured models in tokens: cost per million input tokens, cost per million output, context window size. That unit is now misleading. A token is only worth what it contributes to a finished task, and the gap between models on that measure is far wider than the gap on benchmark accuracy.

The GDPx results published this week make the point concretely. Gemini 4 Carbon scored 10.9% against Claude Opus 5.5 at 12% — a difference most buyers would call a tie — but cost $16.29 per task against $51.41. On a thousand-task workload that is a rounding error versus a budget line. Carbon also beat its own predecessor Argon by more than three points, so the improvement is real rather than a pricing trick.

The same reframing explains why the You Don't Need Long Context paper matters more than another million-token announcement. If compaction preserves task performance while cutting cost by up to 36.7x, then a large context window is an expensive convenience, not a capability. Buying context you do not need is like buying storage you never write to.

Cost per task also exposes the part of the stack that benchmarks hide. A coding agent that succeeds 60% of the time but needs three retries costs more than one that succeeds 55% of the time first try. That is why Cognition shipped SWE-2 Flash as a cheap fast variant, and why routing — sending easy work to small models and hard work to frontier ones — is becoming the core engineering skill in AI products.

So the question to ask of any model release now is not what it scores, but what a completed unit of work costs and how often it needs a human to finish the job. The labs that win the next year may not be the ones with the best benchmark line, but the ones with the best cost curve at acceptable reliability.

The State of AI 2026-11-05

The biggest structural news is OpenAI's agreement to acquire Pierre Computer Company, the team behind the Trees, Diffs and Highlights libraries already embedded in the Codex app, plus the Code Storage service. It follows OpenAI's Pando Git backend work and reads as a bet that source control and review, not chat, is where coding agents will be judged.

Open weights had a heavy day. Tencent launched Hy4 with visual understanding and computer use, promising weights and system cards shortly; Moonshot published Kimi K3.1 Pro, a 2.8T-parameter mixture-of-experts with a one-million-token context, on Hugging Face; and Ripple-65B previewed as US open-weight state of the art on agentic coding. vLLM shipped day-0 support for Kimi, which is the signal that matters for anyone planning to actually serve it.

On price, SemiAnalysis's GDPx benchmark put Google's Gemini 4 Carbon within about a point of Claude Opus 5.5 at roughly a third of the cost per task, ahead of GPT-6 Astra. That is the clearest evidence yet that the frontier is converging on quality and diverging on economics.

In India, IndiaAI showed EKA-1, a small low-cost sovereign model, alongside Dhīr at 22B parameters, and Sarvam's Prism coding model continued to appear in Indian model trackers. Meanwhile data-centre expansion took hits at the ballot box in the US, and Google committed $15bn to Malaysian capacity — compute siting is now a political question as much as an engineering one.

Research worth your time: LOLPlay found that higher reasoning effort never improved real-time game agents because longer thinking left fewer interaction rounds, a useful counterweight to test-time compute enthusiasm. The compaction paper claiming 36.7x cost savings, and ReSI-Math's self-generated training signal, are the two others to read.
