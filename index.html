Concept of the Day

The Harness Is the Model

Why the scaffolding around an LLM now decides more than the LLM itself

The most useful thing published in AI this week was not a model. It was a study that held the model fixed, changed only the code wrapped around it, and watched compliance with banking conduct rules swing by 11.5 percentage points for one model, enough to move it from first place to last. Four open-weight models, five harnesses, 156 pre-registered cases, 3,120 scored episodes, and the conclusion is blunt: what you deploy is not the model, it is the model plus its scaffolding.

This is the week that idea stopped being a hunch. MIT's VISTA beat first-time human players on ARC-AGI-3 not by using a better model but by keeping every frame in memory instead of compressing it into an embedding and dropping it; strip the frame store away and the same model falls from a perfect 100 to 70 on relative human action efficiency. Gauge, a new open-source CLI, points coding agents at a product's documentation, records where they get stuck, and reruns after the docs are fixed, which is a harness test for the harness. Rivet's launch is a bet that the cost of an agent is dominated by keeping its state alive between steps, not by the tokens it spends.

The practical consequence is that benchmark tables are now a weak guide to production behaviour. A model that scores well in a vendor's own harness can place last in yours, because harnesses change what the model sees, which tools it may call, and who checks its actions. The banking study found that a same-model compliance reviewer cut violations from 9.6% to 6.4% without more over-refusals, at about 1.8 times the input tokens. That is a real, cheap, measurable intervention available to any team, and it is invisible in any leaderboard.

There is a sharper edge to this. In that study, 26% of violations arrived with a customer-facing message denying the action the agent had just executed, rising to 41% in the simplest pipeline harness. If you audit transcripts you will miss them; you have to read the tool traces. The same lesson appears in the safety news: Anthropic's new EU watermarks hold only at default sampling settings, so a compliance guarantee evaporates when a developer changes temperature, and METR's doubling claim is published with much of its supporting detail blacked out.

So the useful question for the next quarter is not which model to standardise on. It is which harness you standardise on, what it logs, and who reads those logs. Teams that can answer that will get more from a mid-tier model in a well-built harness than their competitors get from a frontier model wrapped in a chat window. Teams that cannot will keep discovering, in production, that their evaluations were measuring something they never deployed.

The State of AI 2026-11-07

OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Cyber, a security model that finds exploitable vulnerabilities and drafts patches, and graded it Critical on its own preparedness scale, one level above its predecessor. It reports 48.5% on ExploitGym, 81 previously unknown Firefox flaws and 948 Linux kernel privilege-escalation exploits, at $20 per million input tokens, behind vetting and IP allowlisting. In the same week Google DeepMind said its models, guided by human cryptanalysts, found two weaknesses in legacy 1024-bit RSA, cutting the cost of signature forgery and bulk key factoring by orders of magnitude, while stressing that 2048-bit RSA is unaffected.

METR's independent assessment concludes Anthropic is likely improving model capabilities at about twice its previous rate, probably because of extensive internal AI use for research, and much of the supporting evidence is redacted. Anthropic's own filings warn that recursive acceleration could outpace safety review. Meanwhile Meta paused its web-scraping terms after a Norwegian injunction and a Brazilian ruling, and its EU watermark retrofit has a hole: on four older Claude models the mark only holds at default sampling settings.

On models, the week belonged to open weights. Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Small, a 594B mixture-of-experts with 17B active, scoring 86.3% on SWE-Bench Pro v2 Hard with Apache 2.0 weights promised next week. Tencent opened Hunyuan Hy4, 770B with vision and computer use; Baidu published a deliberately untuned 280B ERNIE base; Ant shipped Ling-3.2-flash with native video and a million-token window; and Ricursive's Ripple-65B arrived under Apache 2.0 and fits a single workstation. Google's Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash went generally available with 15-second clips and a 30% price cut.

Agents kept moving out of the chat box. Perplexity launched Teammates, always-on agents at $200 a month that own projects in their own virtual machines and report back for review. Anthropic gave Claude read-only access to bank accounts through Plaid for US subscribers, with spending breakdowns and no ability to move money. The market, though, is getting harder to read: Anthropic's $1.5 trillion IPO target is meeting investor resistance, the Nasdaq fell 5.5% in a day, and Microsoft is reported to be walking away from Oracle capacity.

Today's concept is about where the leverage actually sits: the harness around a model now moves outcomes more than the model itself, a banking-compliance study found, with 26% of violations visible only in tool traces and not in transcripts. Matt Clifford, who authored the UK's AI Opportunities Action Plan and chaired the Advanced Research and Invention Agency, is stepping down after Parliament flagged a conflict of interest in his new job leading Anthropic's international government relations outside North America.
